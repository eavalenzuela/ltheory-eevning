# Developer tools

Everything here runs from the repository root after `python3 configure.py build`.

## Is the tree healthy?

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `script/check` | Type check, engine-free specs, and the launcher's exit codes, each paired with a planted failure that must be caught. Exits 0 only if every step did what it should. |
| `script/check --fast` | The same without the steps that open a window. |
| `script/check --on-desktop` | The same with its windows (about fifty, one after another) opened on the desktop. Otherwise they are opened where nobody sees them: see `script/offscreen`. |
| `script/offscreen CMD ...` | Runs a command on a desktop of its own that has no screen (a headless `sway`, on the same graphics card), so that whatever windows it opens are not seen. `script/check`, `script/goldens` and every scripted or hidden run of `bin/launch` go through it by themselves; `docs/harness.md` has the particulars. |
| `script/check --soak` | Adds the soaks: eight seeds of the 400-system strategic sim, each run in memory and again with a save and load every 97 ticks, for four and a half game days; the economy sweep (16 seeds); the faction sweep (32 seeds). About 25 minutes. |

`bin/launch APP` exits 0 only when the app started, no script raised an error and every harness command worked. A crash or failed assertion exits with 128 + the signal number.

## Engine-free code

`bin/ltcore MODULE [ARG ...]` runs a Luau module with no window, no GL and no engine. Only the built-ins and `Host` (files, directories, clock, arguments, exit) exist there. Code under `resource/script/Core` and `Tool` must run in it; the type checker enforces that. The rules for such code are in `docs/design/core.md`.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `bin/ltcore Tool/specs [FILTER]` | Runs every `*.spec.luau` under `Core` and `Tool`. |
| `bin/ltcore Tool/econrun [--galaxy N] [--days N] [--out DIR] [--detail 1]` | Runs the galaxy's economy (400 systems, 30 game days by default) and prints a verdict on each of `Tool/EconProbe`'s measures. `--detail 1` adds every class's price level, every step's use and how people fare by security. `--goods 1` and `--atlas 1` print the goods table and who has what. Options such as `--haul`, `--jump`, `--slack`, `--source` override the atlas's settings for one run. |
| `script/econsweep [--seeds N] [--days N]` | `econrun` on 16 seeds at once, with a scorecard of every measure by seed. The exit test of roadmap stage S2; part of `script/check --soak`. |
| `bin/ltcore Tool/factionrun [--galaxy N] [--history N] [--days N] [--out DIR]` | Runs the strategic simulation (the powers, their wars and the pirates on the galaxy's economy; 400 systems, 30 game days by default) and prints a verdict on each of `Tool/FactionProbe`'s measures. `--news 1` prints wars, peaces and the like as they happen (`--news 2`: sieges, claims and raids too); `--every H` prints a line on the galaxy every H hours; `--detail 1` adds a table of the powers, their relations, where every credit came from and went, and the counters. `--out DIR` writes the hourly series, the news and the verdicts. |
| `script/factionsweep [--seeds N] [--days N]` | `factionrun` on 32 seeds at once, with a scorecard. The exit test of roadmap stage S3; part of `script/check --soak`. |
| `script/sweep TOOL NAME ...` | What the two sweeps are made of: runs a tool over seeds in parallel into `cache/NAME/sweep-<time>/` and prints one row per check, one column per seed. `--rescore DIR` prints the scorecard of an earlier sweep. Exits 1 if any check failed. |
| `bin/ltcore Tool/simdiverge [--galaxy N] [--history N] [--ticks N]` | Runs the strategic simulation twice, once with a save and load every 97 ticks, and finds the first tick and the first fields where the two differ. |

To change how the powers behave, edit `resource/script/Core/Data/Powers.luau` (every number of the faction layer, each with what it does), run `factionrun --detail 1 --news 1` on a seed or two to see the effect, then the sweep. A sweep measure that misses is a reason to look at a seed with `--detail`, not to move the limit.

## The game

`bin/launch game` runs the game: the Core shown by the engine (`resource/script/App/game.luau`, `resource/script/Game/`). The engine hides the system's pointer and the game draws its own, for a person; a scripted run has none (its pictures must not depend on where the mouse is) unless it asks with `GameDebug.Pointer(true)`. Left-drag turns the camera and the wheel zooms; click a bracket or an overview row to select, then use the orders on the selected-item panel. It opens on a menu (continue, new game from a seed, load, quit); Escape brings up the menu in play (resume, save, load, main menu, quit). P pauses, F9 saves to `save/quick/`, F10 loads it, and docking saves to `save/auto/`.

`GameDebug` is a global table for the console and for scripted runs: `Order(kind, what, range)`, `Select(what)`, `Docked()`, `InSpace()`, `InWarp()`, `Idle()`, `System()`, `Distance(what)`, `TargetGap()`, `Doing()`, `TimeScale(n)`, `Zoom(radii, yaw, pitch)`, `Save(slot)`, `Load(slot)`, `Hash()`, `Mark(name)` and `Same(a, b)`, `Jumps(n)` and `JumpsLeft()`, `Memory()`, `Vram()`. `what` is a kind (`"belt"`, `"station"`, `"gate"`, `"asteroid"`, `"planet"`) or part of a name, and means the nearest match.

In the game, `GameDebug.LookAt('sun')` (or any name the overview knows) swings the camera to look just past the ship at it. `GameDebug.LookFrom('sun', zoom, turn)` puts the camera on that side of the ship instead (its lit side, for the sun), swung round by `turn` radians. `GameDebug.Fly(type)` is a cheat for looking at hulls: while docked, a hull of that type from nowhere, boarded whatever the pilot's skills, with the weapons its design is drawn with (as far as they fit) loaded. `GameDebug.Arms()` says how many weapon models stand on the mounts of the pilot's ship (two to a fitted weapon: a hardpoint is a pair of mounts), and how far from its target the worst-pointed of the turrets that are firing still is.

`script/viewer` runs the viewer: one window that goes through a list of views of a body (in `App/viewer.luau`) and saves a picture of each to `cache/viewer/shots`. It is meant to be started once and left, minimised if wanted, so that no window is opened per picture. `script/viewer start [FILTER]` opens it and takes the views; `script/viewer again [FILTER]` reloads every shader and takes them again (a few seconds); `script/viewer sheet PREFIX` puts the pictures named `PREFIX*` side by side in `cache/viewer/sheet-PREFIX.png`; `script/viewer stop` closes it. With a FILTER only the views with that word in their names are taken (`moon`, `gas`, `star-red`, `rings`, `fx-` for weapons firing, `hull-` for every hull that has a design, from six sides and with its design's weapons on its hardpoints, `grown-` for what the engine's first hull generator makes in the game's paint (`Hull.Grown`: twelve seeds as it grew a frigate, `grown-4`, and twelve as it grew its biggest hulls, `grown-6d`) and `old-` for that generator's own hulls as the engine makes them, bare, at three sizes (a radius of 44, 110 and 160 m), `arms` for every weapon model and the three sizes side by side, `wreck-` for each hull's wreck, `base-` for the control towers, the station modules and whole stations in their fields, ...). While it waits it sleeps, but close it before timing the game: two programs share the graphics card. `cache/viewer/params.txt` overrides the settings of the kind of body being looked at, one a line: a name and a number (`seaLevel 0.5`), or three for a colour (`rockPale 0.6 0.5 0.4`). Shaders and settings are reloaded on every pass; a change to a script needs `stop` and `start`. A scripted run can hold one view still instead, with nothing read from those files: `bin/launch viewer --do "lua assert(Viewer.Show('gallery')); frames 40; shot golden; quit"`. The golden images `bodies` (every kind of body in one picture) and `effects` (every weapon landing its shot) are taken that way.

A scripted run opens a window that stays black: `--no-present` means frames are drawn and saved but never shown. To watch an app, run it without the harness flags.

A script works the station screens by pressing buttons by name, which runs the same code a click does. A button's name is its label (`UNDOCK`, `Market`, `Skills`) or, where several look alike, an id: `Fit:<type>`, `Unfit:<rack><slot>`, `ToHangar:<type>`, `Refine:<type>`, `Train:<skill>`, `Learn:<skill>`, `Board:<n>`, `Assemble:<type>`, `BuyNow`, `SellNow`, `PlaceSell`, `PlaceBuy`, `StartJob`, `Runs:most`, `Quantity:100`, `Research:material`, `Research:time` (and `GameDebug.Researched(type)` gives the levels learned of a blueprint). The screen is a frame behind the game, so wait for the button rather than asserting it: `until GameDebug.Press('Fit:miner.small')`. `GameDebug.CanPress(name)` and `WhyNot(name)` say whether a button is live; `Enter(field, n)` types into a number field (`Quantity`, `SellPrice`, `BuyPrice`, `Runs`); `Market(type)` and `Blueprint(type)` open the market or industry tab at a row; `Info(type)` opens the information window on a type (as pressing `Info:<type>` does, the name of every item link and Info button; `Info:close` and `Info:back` are its own buttons). At a starbase of the pilot's own the screen has a `Starbase` tab, with `Fuel:hold` and `Fuel:hangar` (and `Fuel:corporation`, for fuel in the corporation's hangar there), `Anchor:<module>` and `Unanchor:<module>`; in space a selected moon has `Anchor tower`; `GameDebug.Tower()` says how the pilot's newest starbase stands (`"none"`, `"anchoring"`, `"dark"` or `"lit"`), its fuel, and how many of its modules are online. The colonies window has `Colonies` (the button that opens it), `Colony` (a selected planet's), `Colony:close`, `Colony:next`, `Colony:deploy`, `Extractor:<raw>`, `Processor:<product>`, `Storage`, `Programme:<facility>:<hours>`, `Remove:<facility>` and `Take:<type>`; `GameDebug.Colony(what)` opens it on a planet, `Deposit(n)` names the nth raw material of the planet shown and its product, and `Colonised(type)` says how many facilities the colony shown has (-1 with no colony), how many are working, and how many of a type its store holds. For the economy there are also `Hangar(type)`, `Skill(id)`, `Training()`, `Jobs()`, `Orders()`, `Ships()`, `HoldFull()`, `Mine()`, `Running()` and the cheat `Grant(credits)`, which is entered in the wallet journal as a cheat. `Order(kind, 'home')` means the pilot's home station. A person starting the game is given a menu and a script is not (`Host.scripted`); `GameDebug.Menu('main')` or `Menu('pause')` opens one as a person would get it, and `Menu()` says which page is up. Its buttons are `Menu:continue`, `Menu:new`, `Menu:load`, `Menu:save`, `Menu:resume`, `Menu:main`, `Menu:quit`, `Menu:back`, `Menu:start`, `Menu:random`, `Load:<slot>`, `Save:<slot>` and `Delete:<slot>`, and the seed field is `Seed`. `GameDebug.Seed()` is the galaxy's seed. A script's saves (`GameDebug.Save`, the menu's) are kept in `cache/test-saves`, a person's in `save/`. `Assets` opens the assets window (`Assets:close`). `Map` opens the star map (`Map:close`, `Map:factions`, `Map:security`, `Map:destination`, `Map:clear`, `Map:safest`, `Map:autopilot`), and `Autopilot` is the switch at the foot of the screen once there is a destination. `GameDebug.NewGame(seed, systems)` begins a game as the menu does; `Galaxy()` gives the number of systems and whose the pilot's is; `Destination(system, safest)` sets where to go (a name or a number) and returns the jumps, and with no argument the jumps left; `Autopilot(on)` switches it; `Far()` names the farthest system that is not lawless, for somewhere to send it. (Two `until GameDebug.Press(...)` lines one after the other can run in the same frame, and the second press replaces the first: wait for what the first press does in between.) The overview's `Scanner` button opens the scanner window (`Scanner:close`, `Scan`, `SiteWarp:<n>` for the nth site listed); `GameDebug.Sites(kind)` gives how many sites the system has, how many are found, and the name of the first found one of a kind; a found site is on the overview as kind `site`. The Missions tab has `Accept:<n>` (the nth job offered), `Complete:<n>` and `Abandon:<n>` (the nth in hand); `GameDebug.Missions()` gives how many jobs the station offers, how many are in hand, how many have been finished, and the standing with the station's faction. A script's run is silent; `GameDebug.Sound(true, 0)` switches sound on at no loudness, so that its code runs, and `Sound()` gives how many sounds have been started (the menus' switch is `Menu:sound`). `GameDebug.Wreck()` is a cheat that leaves a pirate scout's wreck a kilometre off the pilot's ship, and `Salvage()` switches every salvager on, on the nearest wreck. `GameDebug.Give(type, n)` and `Train(skill, level)` are cheats for runs that test something other than buying and training; `Held()` gives the share of its speed an entanglement projector leaves the pilot's ship and whether it is kept out of warp, and `Held('target')` the same for the first ship locked (`Fire()` switches on everything that takes a target, these included). `GameDebug.Traffic()` gives how many other ships the system has, how many are on the pilot's grid, and how many ships on the overview would fire on the pilot; `Standing(faction)` gives what a faction thinks of the pilot, `Marked()` says whether the patrols take the pilot for a criminal, and `Battery()` how many volleys of a station's batteries the scene has shown. `GameDebug.Security()` gives the security of the pilot's system and its band (`"secure"`, `"low"`, `"lawless"`), and `Pirates()` the number of pirates on the pilot's grid and how many of them the scene draws as a hull of the pirates' own with its weapons on. For fights, `GameDebug` also has `Lock(what)`, `Locked()`, `Fire()`, `Count(kind)`, `Hold(type)`, `Fitted(type)`, `Condition()`, `Wallet()`, `Hull()`, `Peaceful(on)` and `SelfDestruct()`. The career agents are a tab of the screen at the station a pilot starts at (`Careers`) and a page of the guide's window anywhere (`Guide`, then `Guide:lessons`): `Career:<id>` picks an agent out (`flight`, `industry`, `trade`, `combat`, `survey`), `Lesson:accept:<id>` takes their next lesson and `Lesson:report:<id>` reports it done; `GameDebug.Career(id)` gives how many of an agent's lessons are done, whether the next is in hand, how much of what it wants is done and of how much, and whether it can be taken or reported now. `test/scenario/careers.txt` (`script/check`: `game-careers`). For what is seen: `GameDebug.Passing()` gives how many ships the scene is drawing that are in warp (not the pilot's own); `Leaving()` names a ship on the pilot's grid that is turning to its warp, sets the camera to follow it, and says whether the one it named before is still drawn, in warp; `Turned(what)` gives how far a planet or moon has turned on its axis, in turns, and how many worlds of the system turn; and `Next(why)` gives how many places of the system are picked out as where the pilot is next to go (`Game/Waypoints`), and the name of the first for a reason (`"route"`, `"job"`, `"lesson"`). A double click is two harness clicks a few frames apart (`click Left X Y`, `frames 4`, `click Left X Y`). `test/scenario/sights.txt` (`script/check`: `game-sights`).

## Looking at the game

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `bin/launch APP --console` | A Luau prompt in the terminal that started the app: type a line, it runs in the app's VM. The harness command `lua CODE` does the same from a script. |
| `script/goldens [NAME ...]` | Runs the golden-screenshot scenarios and compares each with its stored image. |
| `script/goldens --update [NAME ...]` | Stores this run's screenshots as the golden images. |
| `bin/launch hulls` | The hull browser (`App/hulls.luau`), in a window, for a person: the hulls the engine's first generator makes (`Item/ShipType/Generate`), one at a time by seed. Left and Right step the seed, Down and Up ten at a time, R picks one at random, B has only the broad ones stepped to (most of what it grows is long and thin), `[` and `]` change the size (1 to 8: how many plates it grows, and from 4 up a deck under each), P goes round the paints (bare, as the engine made them, then the game's: civil, vessari, talmar, kethri, vail, unbound), S goes round the styles (`old`, the generator's own numbers, then `stout`, `broad`, `tall`, `spear`, `slab`, `soft`: other sets of them, `Hull.STYLES`, which make other families of hulls from the same seeds), F shows it as a ship (a frigate's guns and engines on it, then a destroyer's, a cruiser's, a battleship's, then none: the engines on the ends the hull has astern, the guns where the game's rule of hardpoints lets them stand, and it says so if the hull has not the room), K writes the one shown to `cache/hulls-kept.txt`, Space stops it turning; a right drag turns it and the wheel moves in and out. |
| `script/hulls seeds [FROM [COUNT [SIZE]]] [PAINT] [CLASS] [STYLE]` | A sheet of COUNT seeds from FROM on (40 from 1) at a size (2), each with its seed written on it, taken off the screen; the sheet's name is printed (`cache/hulls/sheet-...png`). About a second a hull. The numbers come first and the words after them in any order: a paint (bare if none), a class (`frigate`, `destroyer`, `cruiser`, `battleship`: each hull is then shown as a ship of it, "no room" is written on those that cannot carry its hardpoints, and how many can is printed) and a style (`old` if none). |
| `script/hulls broad [FROM [COUNT [SIZE]]] [PAINT] [CLASS] [STYLE]` | The same of only the broad hulls: those at least a third as wide as they are long. |
| `script/hulls sizes SEED [PAINT] [STYLE]`, `script/hulls paints SEED [SIZE] [STYLE]`, `script/hulls styles SEED [SIZE] [PAINT] [CLASS]` | One seed at each of the eight sizes, in every paint, or in every style. |
| `resource/hull/seeds.txt` | The hulls of the game that are drawn by seed: a hull's type, a seed and a size to a line, and a style if it is not the generator's own (`frigate.prospector 12 2`, `frigate.courier 7 2 stout`). A hull named there is drawn as the generator grows that seed, in its own paint, with its engines and its hardpoints put on by rule, in place of the design drawn for it; its tech 2 make and the pirates' copy of it follow. If the seed has not the room for the hull's hardpoints the game says so as it starts and draws the hull as it was. |

`bin/launch APP --do "COMMANDS" --out DIR` drives an app with scripted input and saves screenshots; see `docs/harness.md`.

A script drives the hull browser with `Hulls.Show(seed, size, paint, class, style)` (the hull, still, from off the bow, with nothing written over it, as a ship of a class if one is named and in a style if one is; false if there is no such size, paint, class or style), `Hulls.Look(round, above, off)` (from somewhere else: how far round from the bow and how far above, in turns of a circle, and from how many of its lengths off), `Hulls.Fitted()` (the class it is shown as, whether it has the room for that class's hardpoints, and how many engines it was given), `Hulls.Now()` (the seed, size and paint shown, the plates grown for it and how wide it is for its length) and `Hulls.Broad(from, count, size, style)` (the first so many broad seeds from one on, with commas between). A hull is what its seed, size and style make it whatever the paint: the bare one in the `old` style is the generator's own model, and every other is the same draws through `Game/View/Hull` (`Hull.Grown`, and `Hull.GrownBare` for bare metal; the two agree on a hull's bounds to a part in a thousand), `GameDebug.HullSeed(type, seed, size, style)` does for one hull, in a running game, what a line of `resource/hull/seeds.txt` does (a ship already in the scene keeps its looks until it is drawn again: undock, or board another; it gives false and why if the seed has not the room). `Hull.GrownShape` gives how far one reaches each way without making it, and `Hull.GrownShip` is one as a ship: engines let into the faces it has astern with nothing behind them (the biggest of those in its aftmost third), and each hardpoint a place on top of a plate with one beneath, or a place on a starboard face with its twin to port, each taken as far as can be from those taken before, where the whole foot of the weapon has plate under it (`Plates.Rests`, asked through the hull's bend) and nothing of the hull over it.

## Models

Most hulls are built in script, plate by plate (`Game/View/Hull`). A hull can also be read from a file somebody modelled: a Wavefront `.obj` (Blender: File, Export, Wavefront) or a binary glTF `.glb`, under `resource/model`.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `script/modelgen [DIR]` | Writes the models that are made from numbers (the Vail's hulls, from the owner's drawings in `docs/concepts/vail`) as `.obj` files with their `.mtl`: Y up, the bow along -Z, a unit long, an object to a part and a material to a paint. Needs nothing but Python; Blender opens what it writes. |

A model can also be built on a shape made elsewhere: the generator reads the hull of a `.glb` under `resource/model/.../base`, cuts the hull's bands cleanly across its faces, and sets its own detail on its back by where the surface is (the Vail's battleship). A design can name parts that stand on a hull and are not to shelter it (`apart = { "bridge" }`): how open each place is to the sky is worked out without them, so the hull about them is not darkened.

What the game takes from a model file (`Game/View/Imported`, on the readers in `Tool/Mesh`) is its shape and the names of its parts. The shape is turned to lie as a hull does (a design says which way the file's bow and back are, if not -Z and +Y), set to the hull's size, and its sharp edges kept sharp. Each part gets one of a hull's six paints: its material's, if that is named `plating`, `patch`, `frame`, `lit`, `trim` or `fire`; else a guess from the part's names (an engine's glow is its fire, a light or a window is lit, a nozzle is trim), from whether its material gives off light, and from its colour (bright and strong is lit, strong is plating, grey is the other plating, near black is trim). Engines are where the fire is; a model with nothing painted as fire can have its design name a word in the names of the parts that are its engines' mouths (`exhausts = "cooling ring"`). A part that is inside out is turned about on the way in, where that can be told: a closed part that holds less than nothing, or an open sheet whose faces plainly look toward the ship's middle line (a part mirrored across a ship in Blender and never given its normals back is the usual case; in Blender, Mesh, Normals, Recalculate Outside mends it at the source). The hull's own shader then paints, seams, wears and lights it as it does every hull; pictures laid on a model, and anything that moves, are not read.

Gun mounts are not in the file. A hull's design (`designs` in `Game/View/Hull`) says where along and across the hull each hardpoint is, and `Imported.Mounts` puts a mount on the back and one on the belly there, standing as the surface about it does. `Hull.Audit` holds them to the same rule as every other hull: the whole foot of the gun has to have hull under it.

To add a hull from a file: put it under `resource/model`, and give its type a design whose `draw` is `vessel("folder/file.glb", { fore = "-x", up = "+z" })` and whose `place` returns its hardpoints. `script/viewer start hull-NAME` shows it.
