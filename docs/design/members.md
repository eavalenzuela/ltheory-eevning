# Members as people: the plan

Planning only (the owner, 2026-11-21): nothing here is built until the owner says so.

## Why

The owner, 2026-11-21: "joining your corp is good, but right now they feel like they are just numbers in the background, not actual corp members. They don't queue their own skills, change ships, buy stuff, join fleets, etc etc. obviously this is a big set of features, so I think it's worth its own planning phase."

Today (`Core/Assets/Members`, `Core/Tactical/Fellows`) a member is a trade, a rung on a fixed ladder of hulls, and a wealth figure. Earnings come in as a number and the tax goes out. Nobody buys, fits, trains or sells anything real. Their ship is seen only while they work in the pilot's system. Only the owner's own characters have skills, and those skills only raise earnings.

What follows from them:

- **Room in the corp:** 20, and 20 more for each level of Hospitality: 120 at level 5; held systems (3 each) and towers (2 each) still add to that. This replaces today's 4 + 3 a held system + 2 a tower (at most 40) + 2 a level of Hospitality.
- **Fleet size:** 10, and 10 more for each level of Leadership: 60 at level 5. The fleet is made of corp members; today Leadership counts hired pilots (one a level, plus the corp's staff places).
- **No more hired pilots.** What they do today moves to members in the pilot's fleet: following the pilot, warping and jumping with them, fighting, mining the pilot's belt, hauling between the pilot's hangars, and going through a portal after the pilot.
- **A fleet screen.** The pilot invites members to the fleet, and gives each one a ship from the corp hangar to fly, from a list that shows only the ships whose hull and fit that member can fly and use. (A member asked in with no ship given flies their own.)
- **Titles.** The pilot grants titles; a title gives no privileges yet, but some will let a member do certain things: a Fleet Commander will lead fleets of members to attack enemy targets (at war, in held systems, and so on).

## The two depths

Both kinds of member have a wallet (except as below), a home station, a hangar there, real ships with real fits, a clone and implants, and the drives.

- **The owner's own characters.** They have their real skills from their character file. The owner sets a skill plan and a ship or fit to aim for. They buy for themselves within a budget the owner sets for each. They never leave the corp.
- **NPC members.** They have a grade instead of skills: it decides what they can fly and how well they do their trade, and it rises with time at that trade. They are paid a share per haul into their own wallet and spend it as their drives say. They can leave.

## A member's life

### Away from the pilot (coarse)

About once a game hour, each member takes one step:

1. **Work.** They spend the hour at their trade, in a system where the corp has a presence or that a call names. What they bring in is real goods: ore, salvage, loot or bounties. The goods go into the corp hangar at the corp station nearest that system, and the member is paid their share from the corp wallet.
2. **Risk.** Each hour away carries a chance of loss. The chance depends on how safe the space is and how strong their ship is for the work, and is lower in an op with others. A loss is as for the pilot: ship, capsule perhaps, clone, implants. It goes into the activity log and corp chat.
3. **Spend.** Between sessions, at their home station, they buy what they need at that station's real market. This takes market stock, so it moves prices. They buy ammo and charges to stay stocked, a better hull or modules when they can afford their next step or their goal, skill books, and implants once they have money to spare. They fit the ship for their trade from the same fits the game's other NPCs use.
4. **Train.** The owner's characters train their real queue, as set by the owner. NPC members' grade rises with time at their trade.

### Near the pilot (real)

Where the pilot is, a working member flies their actual ship, with their actual fit, in space, as members already do today (`Tactical/Fellows`). Today that ship is a rung on the ladder; under this plan it is the ship they own. A loss here is a real wreck.

## Fleets and ops

- **In the pilot's fleet.** The pilot asks for members to join (a fleet call). Members who are free and willing come in their own ships, then follow fleet orders as hired pilots do: warp, approach, target, mine. They leave when the op drags on, their ship is badly hurt, or pay is owed.
- **Their own ops.** A member who leads, by rank or goal, starts an op: a mining op, a pirate hunt or a convoy, in a system. Others join it. The pilot sees it on an Ops list and can warp in to join.
- **Scheduled ops.** The pilot posts an op with a kind, a place and a time. Members sign up by how free and how willing they are, and gather when the time comes.

## Drives

These sit alongside their trade and getting rich.

- **Rank.** Titles in the corp, which the owner grants; no privileges yet (a Fleet Commander will lead fleets against enemy targets).
- **Loyalty.** It grows with time in the corp, ops flown together and pay that arrives on time, and makes a member slower to leave over the tax or thin earnings.
- **Personal goal.** Each member has one, for example "fly a battlecruiser", "a fortune of 500 million" or "the best miner in the corp". The goal steers what they buy and, for NPC members, how fast their grade rises. A goal reached goes in the log and the chat.

## How members show

- **Profile page**, one per member: their name and corp rank; their skills (the owner's characters) or grade; their ships and fits; their wallet; their goal and how far along it they are; what they're doing now; and their recent history.
- **Activity log**, on the corp side: what members did. For example, "bought a Pithis", "lost a Mule to pirates in Okara", "delivered 40,000 m³ of ore", "reached their goal".
- **Corp chat**: short lines from members as things happen, such as heading out, a good belt found, a lost ship, joining an op, or pay being late. No more than a few an hour.

## Phase 1, built (2026-11-21)

In `Core/Assets/Members` (save version 92) and the Corporation window:

- Every member has a home (the corporation's station where they work, or the pilot's home), a clone there, the hulls they own, a grade (NPC members: 1 to 5, rising at 150, 400, 900 and 2,000 hours worked, each grade above the first bringing in 10% more and opening a hull class: cruisers and barges at 2, battlecruisers and freighters at 3, battleships at 4, capitals at 5) or their skills (the owner's characters), what they want (the best hull of their trade, a fortune of 25, 60 or 150 million, or to bring in the most of their trade), loyalty (10% on joining, 2% more a day; up to 10 points more tax borne and 4 more lean days at the most), a title slot, and what they are owed.
- Miners put the ore they dig, and fighters half of what they bring in as salvage, into the corporation's hangar at its station where they work, and are paid the share (60%, set on the Members page) of what it goes for there. Fighters' bounties, haulers' trade and scouts' pay are money as before, taxed. The owner's characters are paid nothing, and all their money goes to the corporation. A member the corporation (and the pilot for it) cannot pay is owed; one still owed at the end of a day leaves (NPC members only).
- Room: 20, 20 more a level of Hospitality, 3 a held system, 2 a tower.
- The Members page: the share beside the tax, grade in the trade column, what each has brought in; a member's row opens their page (grade and hours, home, what they are doing, loyalty, what they want and how far along, the hulls they own, wallet, owed, brought in and paid, ships lost, and their lines of the log). The Activity page: the corporation's log, newest first.
- Saves of version 91 load with each member made a person (a grade as their hull needs, at the pilot's home, their hull theirs, loyalty 30%).

## Phase 2, built (2026-11-21)

- Each member shops once a day at their market (the station they live at where it has one, else one in the system they work in, else the pilot's home), paying what it asks and taking it out of its stock (`Market.Draw`): the next hull of their trade (as before, with a fifth over, twice over for one set on a fortune; only a hull their grade or skills let them fly), then the modules their trade wants on the hull they fly, a module at a time as far as their money goes (a miner a mining laser on every turret, a barge's the big ones; a fighter the standard fit's guns and tank, `Core/Fits`; a hauler up to three cargo extensions; a scout an afterburner), and a fighter's charges when down to 400 (up to 2,000; 120 an hour fired, missiles a tenth of that).
- Without the whole fit they bring in less (half, with none of it), and a fighter with nothing to fire half again. One who joins comes fitted out with a full load; a lost ship takes its fit and charges with it.
- Their ships where the pilot is carry what they bought (`Tactical/Fellows`).
- The owner's own characters: a budget (credits of the corporation's they may spend, set on their page, used up as they spend it) and a hull to fly (picked from their trade's hulls). Each day they buy the books of the skills that hull and its prerequisites want that they do not know (learned in their character, everywhere), then the hull once their skills let them fly it, then its fit. "Train for it" puts every level that hull wants into their one training queue, prerequisites first. Their skills in the game are brought up to date from their character every half minute.
- Save version 93: members of before are fitted out as their trade wants, with a full load.

## Phase 3, built (2026-11-21)

- At work where the pilot is not, each hour has a chance of losing the ship: a fighter 0.05%, 0.25% or 0.5% an hour in secure, low and lawless space, a miner 0.02%, 0.15%, 0.3%, a hauler 0.02%, 0.12%, 0.25%, a scout 0.02%, 0.1%, 0.2%; twice that with none of their fit, a tenth less for each grade over the first (to half at most). Where the pilot is, their ships are real and are lost as before.
- A loss is as the pilot's: what was on it and in its hold goes; its insurance pays 40% of the hull's base price (to the member, or to the corporation for the owner's own characters); the capsule goes too one time in five in low security and two in five in lawless space, and then they wake in their clone at home with their implants gone. They fly another hull they own of their trade no better than the one lost, or buy one at their market, or are given the first of their trade.
- With money to spare (twice the price of their next hull, or any once at the top), a member buys an implant for each empty place in their head (each adds 2% to what they bring in), and a fighter a mild booster for the day (5% more) where the market has one.

## Phase 5, built (2026-11-21)

- An op is a mining op (miners), a pirate hunt (fighters) or a convoy (haulers and fighters), in a system the corporation has a place in, beginning so many hours on (48 at most, on the hour) and running so many (1 to 12).
- The pilot posts one on the Corporation window's Ops page. The members of its trades who are free (not signed up for another, not in the fleet) sign up there and then, each with a chance of 40% and 50% more for the most attached (the owner's own characters always).
- A member who could lead (grade 3 or better, attached half or more) posts one of their own trade, one day in ten, in one of the corporation's places, one to three hours on, for three hours; others sign up as above.
- When an op begins, those signed up go to its system and work there until it is over: a fifth more is brought in, and the risk away from the pilot is halved. What it brings in is counted, and said in the log when it ends. Six ops at most are planned or running at once; one over stays listed two days.
- The Ops page lists them (running, planned, over), with who leads each, when, how many signed up and what it brought in; the way there (Set course) to go and fly with them; and the pilot's own can be called off (a member's is theirs).

## Build order (proposed)

Each phase can be published by itself.

1. **The person.** The member record grows a wallet, a home station and hangar, owned ships, a clone, a grade or the character's skills, a goal, loyalty and a rank slot. Deposits go into the corp hangar and pay a share per haul, replacing the abstract earnings and the tax. Old saves are converted. This phase also brings the profile page and the activity log.
2. **Buying, fitting and training.** Members buy from real markets and fit their ships. Goals steer what they buy. The owner can set an own character's skill plan and ship target. NPC members' grade rises.
3. **Losses.** Risk while away, insurance, the capsule, the clone, implants and boosters.
4. **The fleet screen.** Hired pilots go (old saves' become NPC members of their grade, their ships into the corp hangar); members are invited into the fleet, given ships from the corp hangar that they can fly and fit, follow fleet orders, and leave. Fleet size 10 + 10 a level of Leadership.
   (Room in the corp, 20 + 20 a level of Hospitality, goes in with phase 1.)
5. **Ops.** Scheduled ops, and members' own ops, with an Ops list.
6. **Rank, loyalty and corp chat.** Titles the pilot grants; a Fleet Commander leads fleets of members against enemy targets.
