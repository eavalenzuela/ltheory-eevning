# What is new

What each release brings, the newest first, written for whoever plays it.
`script/publish` puts the first section on the portal page as "In this
release" and the ones under it as "Before that": write a release's
section before publishing it. A section is a line beginning `## ` and
the lines beginning `- ` under it.

## Gate arrivals, hangar bays, a longer skill queue

- Coming out of a stargate now mirrors going in: you start in the black of the far gate's tunnel, already facing out, and fly out through the gate at speed. No more sitting beside the gate turned the wrong way and swinging round.
- You get control back about 3 seconds after coming out of a gate, instead of 5.
- Citadel hangar entrances now look like the way into a hangar: you see into a lit bay behind the air shield, with lights across the ceiling, guide lights running in along the floor and a lit far wall. The shield is a faint sheen with a bright rim, not a blue-grey panel.
- The skill training queue has no limit: queue as many levels as you like.

## Towers without docking

- Nobody docks at a control tower any more. Fly inside its field and you're there: your ship stays in space, inside the bubble, where everyone can see it. "Dock" on a tower is now "Enter field", and your ship stops well inside.
- Everything the tower offers works while you're inside the field: its hangar, the market, fitting, repairs, refining, industry, reactions, fuel, and anchoring or taking off modules. The station windows and the Starbase button open in space, and the top bar says whose field you're in.
- Ships you aren't flying are kept by the tower, out of sight. Switch ships from inside the field: the ship you leave is stowed, and the one you board appears where you were. Fleet members' ships work the same way.
- Towers don't tether you or repair your ship. Only your own side gets in: hostiles are kept out of a lit field, as before.
- A dark tower has no field and its modules give nothing. You can still fuel it and run it from where its field would be, but ships there aren't protected.
- Faction citadels now tether you and other ships they're neutral to while you're inside their reach. Outlaws and pirates aren't tethered. Citadels are still docked in, as before.
- Fleet members haul to and from towers, unload mining there and buy for them without docking. Corporation members whose home is a tower park in its field to rest.
- Old saves: a ship you left docked at a tower is now out in its field, and ships stored there stay stored.

## Corporation members as people

- Members now have a grade from 1 to 5 that rises with the hours they work at their trade. Each grade adds to what they bring in and opens bigger hulls: cruisers and barges at grade 2, battlecruisers and freighters at 3, battleships at 4, capitals at 5. Your own characters use their real skills instead.
- What miners dig and what fighters salvage now goes into the corporation's hangar, at the corp station where they work. The member who brought it in is paid a share of what it's worth there, 60% to start; set it next to the tax on the Members page. Fighters keep their bounties, and haulers and scouts are paid as before, all taxed as before.
- If the corporation (or you, covering for it) can't pay a member's share, it's owed. A member who's still owed at the end of the day leaves; your own characters stay.
- Every member has a home station, owns the hulls they've bought, has something they want (the best ship of their trade, a fortune, or to be the corp's best at their trade), and grows more loyal day by day. Loyal members put up with a higher tax and more lean days before leaving.
- Click a member on the Members page to open their profile: grade and hours to the next, home, what they're doing, loyalty, what they want and how close they are, the ships they own, their wallet, what they've brought in and been paid, ships lost, and their recent activity.
- A new Activity page on the Corporation window logs what members do: joining, deliveries, new ships, losses, grade-ups, goals reached, and leaving.
- Room in the corporation is now 20 members, plus 20 for each level of Hospitality, 3 for each system you hold and 2 for each tower.
- Members buy what they fly with at real station markets, paying the market's price and taking its stock, so their buying moves prices. They buy a better hull when they can afford it and their grade or skills allow, then the fit their trade needs a module at a time: mining lasers for miners, guns and armour or shields for fighters, cargo extensions for haulers, an afterburner for scouts. Fighters also buy ammunition and use it up as they fight.
- A member without their full fit brings in less (half with none of it), and a fighter out of ammunition half again. New members arrive fitted out, and a lost ship takes its fit with it. Members' ships in space carry what they've actually bought.
- Members can lose their ships while working away from you. The chance depends on how dangerous the space is, how well fitted they are and their grade. A loss works as it does for you: insurance pays 40% of the hull's list price, the fit goes with the ship, and in low-security or lawless space the capsule can go too. If it does, they wake in their clone at home and lose their implants. They then fly another hull they own or buy a replacement. Losses show on the Activity page and on their profile.
- Members with money to spare buy implants (each adds a little to what they bring in), and fighters take a combat booster where the market sells them.
- Hired pilots are gone: the fleet is now your corporation's members. On the Crew window, invite members who are free into your fleet, then give each one a ship docked there that they can fly (by grade, or by real skills for your own characters). The fleet holds 10, plus 10 for each level of Leadership (60 at level 5). There's no fleet without a corporation.
- Everything hired pilots did, fleet members do: they follow, warp and jump with you, fight, mine, patrol, guard, haul and run errands. What they bring in and buy belongs to the corporation.
- Fleet members are paid by the hour from the corp's account, their share of what their trade would have earned. They go back to their own work after 12 hours in your fleet, or if they're owed pay. Grades they gain in your fleet stay with them.
- Old saves: each hired pilot becomes a corp member at their grade, still in your fleet. Without a corporation they're let go and their ship goes to your home station.
- The Command career now has you found a corporation (the lesson before pays for it), invite the first pilot at your station who's open to joining, and bring them into your fleet.
- Ops: on the Corporation window's new Ops page, schedule a mining op, a pirate hunt or a convoy in one of your systems, starting a few hours ahead and running for up to 12 hours. Members of the right trades sign up (more loyal ones more often), gather in that system when it starts, and work together, bringing in a fifth more with half the risk of loss. Experienced, loyal members sometimes run ops of their own. The page lists every op with its leader, start time, sign-ups and what it brought in. Use Set course to fly out and join them, and call off your own ops if plans change.
- Titles: give any member a title on their profile page, or make them Fleet Commander with one click. Only Fleet Commander does anything so far: whenever a war force is besieging or claiming one of your systems, or a faction at war with you has a force in one of your systems, your Fleet Commander forms a strike. Fighters sign up, and every hour they're there they wear the enemy force down.
- Corp chat: a new Chat page where members say what they're up to: joining, heading out, finds, signing up for ops, losses, new ships, grade-ups, unpaid shares, strikes, and leaving.
- For your own characters in the corp, the profile page has a budget (how much of the corp's money they may spend) and a ship to fly. They buy the skill books that ship needs, which their character keeps everywhere, then the ship once they can fly it, then its fit. "Train for it" adds every skill level the ship needs, prerequisites first, to that character's one training queue.

## Missiles you can see, and fixes from playtesting

- Missiles in flight now have a small body pointing the way they fly and a flickering rocket flame at the tail, with their trail behind. The flame stays a visible bright point at any distance, so you can follow a missile from your launcher all the way to its target, even when it's flying straight away from the camera.
- A citadel's ring of lights at its tether's reach is now a chain of small pulsing lights, with the brightness running round the ring, instead of giant glowing yellow balls.
- Rotating rings, hoops and dishes on hulls were drawn inside out, which made them dim and wrongly lit. They now catch the light properly.

## Flying through wormholes and jump bridges

- Going through a wormhole is now a passage like a stargate's: the camera follows your ship straight into the black sphere, the dark closes in around you, and you come out of the far end's sphere and brake to a stop about 5 km beyond it. The next system loads in the dark, with no freeze.
- Jump bridges work the same way. From anywhere in the gravity station's field, your ship rises above the station, drops down through its turning rings into the black sphere at its heart, and comes out of the far station's sphere, up or down, clear of its outer ring.
- A wormhole station's wormhole is now in the purple sphere at the station's heart, entered through its rings, not floating 15 km away. Wormholes held open in an existing save move there when it's loaded.
- While you're going through, your ship can't be ordered, locked or hurt, as with a stargate. A wormhole's mass is spent as each ship goes through.
- Looking through a wormhole hides the sun behind it, its glare included.
- The message for a bridge jump now says "Jump bridge", with the distance and the station you came out at.
- Jump drives and jump portals still use the white flash for now.

## Event horizons on the stargates

- Every stargate's mouth now holds a shimmering event horizon: a view of the sky of the system it leads to, bent and wavering like light through water, lit round its rim. Skies come in ten colour families, so a gate to a red system shows red and a gate to a blue one shows blue.
- A ring of ripples runs out from the middle of the horizon whenever a ship goes through, in either direction.
- The Dial's horizon is shut while the gate is at rest and opens from the centre like an iris as the dial spins up for a ship coming through.
- Coming out of a gate, you see the system you're arriving in through the far gate's surface.
- The bright flash as your ship crossed into a gate is gone: the horizon's ripple takes its place.
- The Bell is now a solid dark grey-black metal, without the panel lines that ran at odd angles over its curves.
- Citadels show as "Citadel" in the overview's type column, not "Station".

## New stargates, flying through them, and moving parts on ships

- Going through a stargate is now a passage you watch: from 2.5 km the camera follows your ship in, it crosses the gate's mouth and races into black, and comes out of the far gate's tunnel with the new system ahead of you, braking to a stop about 5 km out. The next system loads behind the black, so there's no freeze at the jump. The interface is hidden on the way.
- While going through, your ship can't be ordered, locked or hurt, from the moment it's in range until it's out of the far gate. The game's clock keeps running.
- Haulers and other traffic now fly in and out of the gates instead of appearing and vanishing beside them.
- Four new stargate designs. The Dial, the standard human gate: a slate-dark ring round a ring of lit glyphs that turns slowly, and faster while a ship goes through. The Gyre, in alien space: a ring in two nested gimbals of burnt-ochre stone that spin up when a ship jumps. The Bell, for the longest tenth of gates: a deep flared collar that's always rolling. The Orrery, the aliens' long gate: a rolling bell inside the Gyre's gimbals. Unclaimed space has a mix of each.
- Ships have moving parts: antennas, dishes, rotors and vanes that fold away in warp, sweep and stand up while scanning, aim with electronic warfare, and change with siege, triage, the industrial core or an open jump portal. Antennas come as round rods, ship masts, a trailing whip and a segmented telescoping rod.
- Fins, wings, canards and spines on some hulls, folding flat in warp.
- The Talmar's hulls are remade, and they have a new frigate, the Ende, built for sensor damping.

## Rapid launchers, a burst jammer, drone and scanning modules, and fleet portals

- Rapid Light Missile Launcher (medium hardpoint) and Rapid Heavy Missile Launcher (large hardpoint): they fire light or heavy missiles more than twice as often as the plain launcher of those missiles, from a smaller load that takes 35 seconds to reload.
- Burst Jammer (mid slot): every 30 seconds, each ship that isn't yours within 10 km has a one-in-four chance of losing all its locks.
- Drone Navigation Computer (mid slot): your drones fly 20% faster. Drone Tracking Enhancer (low slot): your drones track and reach 10% further.
- Ice Harvester Upgrade (low slot): 6% more ice each cycle. The Mining Laser Upgrade does nothing for ice.
- Salvage Tackle Rig: 10% more chance each salvager cycle brings something off. Gravity Capacitor Rig and Scan Rangefinding Array (low slot): the probes you launch are 10% stronger.
- A hired pilot flying an ultracapital with a Jump Portal Generator can open a portal for you: pick a system on the map and press Fleet portal. When a portal is open near you, the map shows it with a Go through button, and your other pilots who are following you go through after you.
- Pirate captains now carry masterwork damage modules as well as masterwork guns.
- Module info windows show what each of the new modules does.

## Capital modules, jump portals, navy hulls, deployables, and every module in every make

- Deployables, put out from your hold with Put out in the inventory, at least 5 km from stations and gates, and picked up again with Pick up once empty. A Field Depot holds 3,000 m3, and a ship beside it can refit from its own hold and the depot's. A Salvage Tractor Post pulls in wrecks within 125 km and empties them into its hold, for two days. A Jump Beacon Buoy is a lit beacon for your jump drives for an hour. Small, Medium and Large Interdiction Buoys hold a 5, 11.5 or 26.5 km interdiction field until you pick them up; not in secure space. All are on the market under Structures.
- Navy hulls: each of the four human peoples sells a navy version of one combat hull of each class, frigate to battleship, in its loyalty store. Each is its plain hull in the people's livery, a fifth tougher in every layer, with a tenth more CPU and powergrid, one more slot (a low slot on armoured hulls, a mid slot on shielded ones), and the plain hull's bonuses.
- XL modules for capitals: shield boosters, extenders, armour repairers and plates, afterburners, microwarpdrives, capacitor boosters, remote repairers and the energy neutraliser, about three times a large one. Only capitals fit them.
- Every carrier, dreadnought, siege dreadnought, supercarrier, ultracapital and capital industrial has a jump drive now, except the Kethri colony ship. Plain freighters still have none.
- Every capital industrial except the Nimia's transport refines ore in its own holds, as the Drowned's Griever did.
- The Jump Portal Generator, for ultracapitals: open a portal from the map's jump drive panel to a system with a lit beacon. For a minute, friendly ships within 5 km of you can go through, on your fuel, while you stay. No capital goes through. Your hired pilots who are following you go through and hold where they come out until you call them back.
- Drones now have a control range: 60 km, and 5 km more for each level of Drones. The Drone Link Augmentor adds 20 km.
- The Cipher Buffer, a mid-slot module that gives you one more code in a hacking buffer.
- New modules: shield amplifiers and armour coatings for each damage type, Shield Boost and Armour Repair Amplifiers, the Missile Guidance Enhancer, Inertial Damper, Warp Field Accelerator, Targeting Amplifier, Signature Dampener, Capacitor Recharge Coil, Grid Diagnostic Unit, Auxiliary Generator, a Small Hull Repairer, the Gravitic Grappler (a short, heavy web), and medium and heavy energy neutralisers. New rigs for missiles, turrets and single-type resistances.
- Every module now comes in every make: custom, tech 2, masterwork and officer, each human people's own, and the pirates' site grades. Only modules that simply work or don't (the cloak, the micro jump drive, the jump beacon and portal generators, the bubble launcher and the Cipher Buffer) have none. Every rig has a tech 2 version.
- Faction weapon bonuses: Nimia cruisers and up carry launchers beside their turrets, and their plasma torpedoes fly 25% faster. Kethri hulls with launchers do 25% more damage with the Vertical Launch Battery. The Drowned's Tridents from their cruisers, battlecruisers and battleships hit 15% harder and are 25% tougher.
- Faction citadels are fought like ships: no shield bubble or batteries, guns and point defence of their own, reinforced between layers, and attacking one costs standing with its faction as attacking their ships does. Destroyed, it leaves its hulk.
- Towers are much tougher (12, 24 or 48 million shield by size), with fields of 15, 21 or 30 km. A citadel can only take so much damage a second however many ships shoot it. A siege you are present for bombards the tower down over about half an hour at the strongest.
- A citadel of yours can be set Closed or Open from its window, with its tax raised or lowered a point at a time.
- Level 1 escort and convoy jobs: each wave of pirates now waits until the one before is beaten.
- Fixed: the factions' makes of weapon upgrades now really make guns fire faster.

## Observatories and citadel miners

- Observatory, for research station citadels: it finds the signals in its system for you, nearest first, each in a minute or so (derelicts nearly two, listening posts two and a half). Found signals show on your overview to warp to, as if you had scanned them down. Its dome's telescope turns and tilts toward whatever it is working on.
- Asteroid Miner, Moon Miner and Planet Miner, for refinery citadels: every hour each brings up, into the citadel's hangar, as much of each thing it mines as a tech 2 mining laser would, times the citadel's size (one for small up to five for huge). The Asteroid Miner mines every ore the system's belts hold, the Moon Miner the material of the moon the citadel is anchored at, and the Planet Miner each raw material of its planet. A Moon Miner fits only at a moon, a Planet Miner only at a planet. Nothing is taken out of the belts.
- A refinery's mining turret turns to its target and holds a beam on it; an asteroid miner draws rocks in on its beam and grinds them away.
- The observatory dome and the mining turret only appear once their module is fitted, and go dark with the citadel.
- Fixed: every citadel and tower had an old-style hull drawn in the middle of it, which showed as a ring of pods around citadels.

## Citadel point defence from its turrets

- A citadel's point defence tracers now come from the turrets that fire them, on its point defence mounts, rather than from its middle.

## Citadels you fight like ships

- Citadels no longer have a shield bubble or outside batteries. A ring of lights marks their tether: your ships within 10 km of one are tethered to it, shown by a beam from the citadel, and cannot be locked.
- A citadel has a shield, armour and structure of its own, much bigger than before. Half its shield is built in, and the rest comes from citadel shield extenders and hardeners fitted in its shield slots (one on a small citadel, up to five on a huge one).
- Shoot a citadel's shield down and it is reinforced for an hour, and nothing can touch it. Then its armour can be fought, and when the armour goes it is reinforced for another hour before its structure.
- One defence module of its size mounts all of a citadel's guns: lasers, cannons, hybrids or missiles, short- and long-range by size. Each gun fires on its own at pirates, the Drowned, anyone who attacks the citadel or your ships, and your war enemies.
- Every citadel has point defence built in, with no module needed: medium autocannons that stream at hostile missiles, drones and fighters while it is lit, and never run out.
- At a citadel of your own, its window is called Citadel and is laid out like the ship fitting window: the citadel turning in the middle, its service, shield and defence slots around it, what goes in the slot you pick on the left, and its fuel, defences, guns and running costs on the right.
- Fixed: ship pictures in two windows at once (the fitting window and an info window) no longer blink and vanish.

## Plasma torpedoes, the Fighter Pylon, and missiles you can outrun

- The Plasma Torpedo Launcher, the Nimia's own, comes in four sizes and fits any ship with a launcher hardpoint. It fires slow, glowing balls of plasma that point defence cannot shoot down. Its own torpedoes come in two kinds: Plasmostatic (90% EM, 10% explosive) and Plasmobaric (90% thermal, 10% kinetic). It fires half as often as the short-range launcher of its size but hits twice as hard.
- The Fighter Pylon, the Drowned's own, carries a Trident fighter on your hull. It takes a turret hardpoint and a lot of CPU, needs Drones 5 and 75 Mbit/s of drone bandwidth, and its Trident counts as one of your drones out. Switch it on and the Trident goes after your locked target; switch it off and it comes back to the pylon. A Trident that is shot down can only be replaced in a station. Drowned cruisers, battlecruisers and battleships carry one each, and cannot replace theirs, so kill their Tridents early.
- Drone bandwidth: every ship now has some, shown in its info window. A drone out uses its volume (5, 10 or 25 Mbit/s), and a fitted Fighter Pylon holds 75. Ships below capital size have as much as their standard drone bay holds, so no ship fields fewer drones than before; capitals have 250. Carriers' fighters and bombers use none.
- Missiles now fly after their targets. A missile that has not reached you when its fuel runs out does no damage, so a fast enough ship can outrun one. Ordinary missiles are quick enough to catch almost anything; plasma torpedoes are slow enough to escape. A missile also comes to nothing if its target warps off or docks.
- Point defence turrets now fire a visible stream of tracers at incoming missiles.
- Level 3 pirate camps can bring a destroyer with two frigates again.

## Swarm missiles, vertical launch, and point defence

- The Swarm Launcher, the Vessari's own, comes in four sizes and fits any ship with a launcher hardpoint. Each cycle it throws its missile as a swarm of small ones: 6, 12, 18 or 24 by size, together doing 90% of the one. They are a third faster and with half the blast, so they hit small ships better. It is short-range, reaching as far as the rocket, assault, torpedo or bombardment launcher of its size, and uses ordinary missiles. Its missiles jink on their way in and leave long glowing contrails.
- The Vertical Launch Battery, the Kethri's own, comes in four sizes and fits any ship with a launcher hardpoint. It ripples eight missiles out of a row of cells, a quarter of a second apart. Each climbs straight up, turns over sharply and dives on its target faster and faster, leaving a thick smoke trail. It reaches 1.3 times as far as the long launcher of its size, but will not fire at anything nearer than a tenth of that. It fires only its own new vertical-launch missiles, which fit no other launcher.
- The Point Defence Turret, a small projectile turret for a turret hardpoint. Switched on, it needs no lock: it shoots at enemy missiles within 5 km, including missiles aimed at your friends, and at enemy drones when no missiles are near. It has to swing to each missile in turn and fires the whole time, so it goes through its 120 rounds fast. Most rounds miss: one turret stops about half the missiles a single launcher throws at it, and a swarm or a vertical salvo overwhelms it.
- Pirates from the level 3 camps up carry a point defence turret in place of one gun. Level 3's hardest pack has one scout fewer, and level 4's lone cruiser now has a scout with it.
- Faction stores sell blueprints only for their own people's weapons and ones belonging to no faction, not other factions' launchers.
- Fixed: destroying a ship with a loaded point defence turret or capacitor booster could break the game.

## Hull bonuses, the Litany Beam, and shield regeneration

- Every faction hull now has bonuses of its own. Most get two that grow with its class skill (Frigates, Cruisers and so on), and some get a role bonus they always have. Each hull's info window lists them under Bonuses.
- Vessari: missile kiters and active armour tankers. The frigates' afterburners give more speed per Afterburners level, the Hoshizora's dampeners are half as strong again, the Manazashi's torpedoes do twice the damage, and the Naishou's turrets track a quarter better.
- Talmar: projectile brawlers with shield boosters, and falloff kiters. Orun: blaster brawlers and railgun snipers. Kethri: lasers that use less capacitor, and beam snipers. Nimia: blaster brawlers whose armour or hull mends faster.
- The short-range hybrid hulls of the Orun and Nimia reach half as far again with a stasis web.
- Unbound (pirate) hulls lock targets half as fast again, and the Cutthroat and Jackal hold ships with warp disruptors from a quarter farther.
- Vail: beam snipers, and hulls that tank by their shield's own recharge. Those make shield regeneration modules work 10% better per level.
- Drowned: cloaked railgun snipers with heavy hulls. Beware the Gravestone destroyer.
- Supercarriers and ultracapitals ignore electronic warfare. Only a capital warp disruptor or an interdiction field holds them.
- New modules for a shield that recharges by itself: the Shield Recharger (mid slot), the Shield Flux Coil (low slot, more recharge for less shield) and the Field Regeneration Rig.
- The Litany Beam, the Vail's own laser, can be fitted by any ship with a laser hardpoint. On a hit, it leaps on to up to two more ships of the target's side nearby, each leap doing half the damage of the one before. It comes from the Vail and is not sold on ordinary markets.
- The Mule hauler, Dray freighter, Prospector and Quarry barge are gone: traders and miners now fly the factions' own ships. Any of them in a saved game becomes a faction ship of the same kind. The Courier stays as the ship you start in.
- Pirate camps: level 3 is always led by a pirate destroyer, level 4 can bring two pirate cruisers at once, and the first-level mining contract wants 200 m3 of ore.

## Modules rebalanced

- Tank modules have new figures. Shield extenders give 400, 800 and 1,900 shield, and armour plates 450, 1,750 and 3,500 armour. Armour repairers mend a little more per second, and shield boosters a little less. Damage control now cuts damage to the hull by 30%, and Reinforced Bulkheads add 15% hull. Large plates, extenders and afterburners are far easier to fit, so a cruiser can carry a large plate.
- Hardeners cost more capacitor to run and more CPU to fit. Capacitor batteries hold 120, 500 and 1,300.
- Damage modules (Heat Sink, Gyrostabilizer, Flux Coil, Guidance Computer) give +7% damage and +8% rate of fire. The Tracking Enhancer and the Tracking, Ranging and Flight Computers give a little less than before. Drone Damage Amplifiers give +15%.
- Electronic warfare is weaker and shorter-ranged. A web halves a target's speed. A Target Painter adds 25% signature, out to 30 km. A Sensor Dampener cuts lock range and scan resolution by about a seventh, at 25 km. Tracking and Guidance Disruptors cut by 15%, out to 40 km. Neutralisers and Nosferatu reach 4 to 16 km by size, and Nosferatu drain little and often. Smartbombs reach 3, 4 and 5 km.
- Remote shield boosters, armour repairers and capacitor transmitters mend more each cycle, but reach only about 10 to 17 km. A capacitor transmitter sends 30 of the 38 it costs.
- Afterburners and microwarpdrives cost more capacitor to run, and a fitted microwarpdrive takes a quarter off your capacitor. Overdrives and Expanded Cargoholds have stiffer drawbacks.
- Mining modules, command bursts, cloaks, ECM and rigs are unchanged.
- Level 3 combat sites are now led by pirate destroyers, with at most one frigate beside one, so a cruiser can take them.

## Ships and weapons rebalanced, citadels across the galaxy, saved fittings

- Every class of ship has new figures: shield, armour, hull, speed, agility, warp speed, signature, targeting, capacitor and hold. Frigates and battleships changed little. Cruisers and attack battlecruisers have less to them; freighters, mining barges, capital industrials and supercarriers a great deal more. A freighter holds 435,000 m3 and a capital industrial's ore hold 250,000 m3; an industrial's hold is smaller, about 2,700 m3.
- Every gun and launcher has new figures, size by size. A bigger gun reaches further and hits harder, but follows a crossing target far worse: a battleship's guns all but miss a frigate. Against smaller ships, use drones, a web or a target painter. A bigger round carries 2, 4 or 8 times what a small one does.
- Damage skills. Laser, Projectile and Hybrid Turrets and Missile Launchers each make their weapons hit 5% harder a level. Rapid Firing now makes every turret fire 4% faster a level (it was autocannons only, and now needs Weapon Upgrades 2). Two new skills: Precision Shooting (turrets hit 3% harder a level) and Missile Tracking (missiles hit 3% harder a level).
- The pilots you fight have skills too: pirates and traders at level 1, empire and corporation pilots at 3, the Drowned at 4, pirate veterans at 3, captains at 4 and named officers at 5.
- Drones keep the weapons they had and are half as tough again. Towers and starbase modules pack smaller, so any industrial still carries one. Courier contracts for a hauler are now up to 1,800 m3 and pay more for each.
- Training is ten times faster: every skill to level 5 in about a week of play. Injectors are unchanged.
- Saved fittings: a new Saved fits tab in the fitting window. Save a ship's fit under a name, and load it onto any ship of that hull at a station: what is in the hangar goes on, charges and all, and what is missing is listed, with a button to buy it on the station's market.
- Citadels have 35 new designs: Habitation, Trade Hub, Refinery, Research Station and Shipyard in all five sizes, a Generator citadel at Large and Extra Large, and the Vail's and the Nimia's own. The gravity and wormhole stations are now the Generator citadel: the generator module you fit makes it one or the other.
- The empires and the alien peoples now have citadels of their own across the space they hold, the more the more secure a system is: in the most secure, about every other planet has a station or a citadel by it. You dock at them and use their services as at any station. When a system changes hands, its citadels fall.
- A fallen citadel leaves a hulk: dark, with half of what was in its hangar and some of its modules to loot, and many cycles of salvage. Right-click a hulk and pick Rebuild to make it yours again, for half the materials of its blueprint, taken from your hold. Hulks also lie in open space, and an alien people's holds a copy of its blueprint, now and then the original.

## Probe scanning, a system map, and places to remember

- A system map: open it from the dock, from a system's panel on the star map, or from the scanner window. It shows the sun, the planets and their orbits, moons, belts, stations, gates, the sites you have found and your ship, from a slant you can turn (right-drag), zoom (wheel) and drag about.
- Real probe scanning. Fit a Probe Launcher (a mid slot; it needs Astrometrics 1) and carry scan probes: Launch puts out up to eight at once, round your ship. On the system map, drag a probe to move it, hold Shift to raise or lower it, or drag the purple ring at their middle to move them all. Set how far they reach, from 0.25 to 64 AU: the smaller, the stronger they read.
- Each scan reads every signal afresh from where your probes are. One probe in reach puts a signal somewhere on a sphere, two on a ring, three at one of two points, and four or more inside a sphere that shrinks as more of it is read. At 100% the site is found. Probes here, beside a signal, sends them all to where it was read.
- The ship's own scanner still finds hidden belts, vapour fields and wormholes by itself. Derelicts and listening posts can only be found with probes. A Probe Launcher II reads a quarter more, and exploration hulls fit a launcher for half the CPU and powergrid.
- Bookmarks: right-click a station, gate, planet, belt or anything else in space and pick Bookmark, or use the Bookmark button on the selected item. Docked, the Places window marks the station you are in.
- The Places window (the pin on the dock) lists your bookmarks by system, nearest first. Go flies there on autopilot and docks or warps to it when you arrive; Route only sets it as your destination; Forget removes it. Pick one to rename it. The star map marks systems with bookmarks.
- Ctrl-click something in space or on the overview to lock it.
- Market goods no longer need standing with a faction: its own makes of modules and its liveries are sold to anybody. Only loyalty store offers still need standing.
- Every station has a Ship Maintenance Array, so you can board, fit and repair ships anywhere, and a ship bought from a contract can be flown away from where it was bought.
- Fitted hulls on contracts now come mostly with better modules than plain: tech 2, a faction's own make, or the pirates' site tier. About one in fifty carries a named officer's module.

## Gravity stations, wormhole stations and a save editor

- Gravity Station: a new kind of citadel, a jump hub. It is a lit jump beacon by itself, with no beacon module needed. It alone fits a Jump Bridge, which sends a ship in its field to another of your gravity stations with a bridge, up to 30 light years away. The station the ship leaves pays the fuel. Jump bridges are on the star map's system panel.
- Wormhole Station: a new kind of citadel. While it has fuel, it holds a wormhole open about 15 km outside itself, leading into uncharted space and already found, and opens another as soon as one closes.
- Wormholes now wear out with mass. Each ship that goes through uses up its hull's mass from the wormhole's budget, and when the budget is spent the wormhole closes behind the ship that spent it. The scanner and overview say whether a wormhole's mass is stable, reduced or critical.
- A fourth size of wormhole, massive, lets capitals through: carriers, dreadnoughts, capital industrials and freighters, but never supercarriers or ultracapitals. It is the rarest size, and the way out that opens when a system's last wormhole closes is always a massive one. So a citadel can now be carried into uncharted space in a freighter.
- Neither jump drives nor jump bridges reach into or out of uncharted space: wormholes are the only way.
- New looks for the medium Refinery and the gravity stations. Wormhole-style citadels open their hangars only on their outer faces.
- A save editor, started with Save Editor.bat in the game's folder on Windows (and from the app list on Linux). Pick a saved game and change credits, skills, standings, loyalty points, items and ships, who holds each system, pirate pressure, structures, wormholes and market stock, or anything else in the save. Close the game first. Folder... picks the saves of another install.

## Citadels, training injectors and twelve new hulls

- Citadels: stations of your own that ships dock inside. Each is one of five kinds (Habitation, Trade Hub, Refinery, Research Station, Shipyard) at one of five sizes, from Small to Huge, and each has its own blueprint. Build one at a station, haul it to a moon or planet and anchor it, then fit service modules in its slots.
- Each kind does its own work better. A Shipyard builds faster and with less material, a Refinery gets more from ore and ice, and a Research Station researches and invents faster and more reliably. A Trade Hub halves the sales tax and broker's fee, and a Habitation has a clone bay and storage without fitting either, and burns less fuel.
- Some modules only one kind can fit. A Shipyard fits the Advanced Ship Assembly, which tech 2 hulls need to be built in a citadel, and from Large up the Capital Ship Assembly, which capitals need. A Research Station fits the Advanced Research Hub, which inventing tech 2 hulls in a citadel needs. A Refinery fits the Reactor.
- What docks at a citadel and what only tethers to it depends on its size. Friendly ships in its field are tethered to it automatically. Undocking ships leave through one of its hangar doors.
- Wormholes are now drawn as lightless spheres, with the stars bent around their edges.
- Training injectors hold banked training time: 1, 6 or 24 hours, added to your training queue at once. Use one from the Skills window's training queue. They are sold at stations, are in every faction's loyalty store, and turn up in data-site vaults, sealed derelicts and now and then a pirate's wreck.
- Twelve new hulls. Talmar: the Nephelo, a black ops frigate, and the Osso, an interdictor. Vessari: the Hoshizora, an electronic warfare cruiser; the Raiden, a cruiser with a bow railgun; and the Manazashi, an attack battlecruiser. Orun: the Excalibur, a tech 2 carrier, and the Guthook, a tech 2 siege dreadnought with eight guns. Kethri: the Gauntlet frigate, the Perfexis cruiser, the Pox dreadnought, the Noxious interdictor and the Deluge, a black ops battlecruiser.
- An interdictor's interdiction field is wider and lasts longer. An electronic warfare hull's webs, disruptors, painters, dampeners, jammers and neutralisers reach farther and work more strongly.
- When a hull has two tech 2 versions (the Ouyou has the Hoshizora and the Raiden), you choose which one to invent.
- Every station has a market again, stocked by its kind.

## Characters

- Your pilot is now a character kept outside any one game. Skills and the training queue go with the character from galaxy to galaxy; the wallet, ships, items, implants, standings and corporation stay with the galaxy they were earned in.
- Every character trains in real time, all of them at once, whether or not the game is running. Running the game faster at a station no longer speeds up training.
- The menus have the same look as the rest of the interface. The main menu shows who you are playing as, with Continue, Characters and games, Options and Quit.
- Characters and games is one screen: your characters down the left, and on the right the one picked, their games grouped by universe (load or delete each) and a new game. New character opens a pane over it: a name, and one of forty-eight portraits or a picture of your own (the path of a .png or .jpg file).
- New game and Continue are the chosen character's; loading a save plays as its character. A save from before characters gives its pilot to a new character the first time it is loaded.
- Options, Graphics has a Look for ships and stations: Standard, as before, or one of two cel-shaded looks drawn in flat tones with inked outlines. Cel V1 keeps the paint's wear and weathering; Cel V2 lays the paint on flat.
- In a corporation's Members page, your other characters can be asked in at a trade. They work as members do, with their own skills making them better at it and deciding which hulls they move up to, and they never leave over tax or thin pickings.

## More modules, and ice

- New modules: a Co-Processor (more CPU) and a Reactor Control Unit (more powergrid); Shield and Capacitor Power Relays; Reinforced Bulkheads; an Adaptive Armor Hardener that shifts its resistance toward the damage you are taking; Capacitor Boosters, which put a capacitor charge's worth into the capacitor each cycle; and Ancillary Shield Boosters and Armor Repairers, stronger or free to run while they have charges or paste. Capacitor charges and nanite repair paste are sold with the other charges.
- More new modules: Drone Damage Amplifiers and Omnidirectional Tracking Links for your drones; Tracking Disruptors and Guidance Disruptors against an enemy's turrets and missiles; Energy Nosferatu, which drain a target's capacitor into your own; Smartbombs of each damage kind, striking everything around you that isn't yours; and Sensor Boosters, your own and remote ones for a friend.
- Utility and industry modules: a Micro Jump Drive (100 km on after a spool); Warp Core Stabilizers, a point each against warp disruption; a Tractor Beam that pulls wrecks in; Ship, Cargo and Survey Scanners, whose readings open in a Scan results window; a Relic Analyzer, which is now what opens a derelict's hold (the Data Analyzer opens listening posts' vaults); Mining Laser Upgrades; and Modulated Mining Lasers and Strip Miners, which take a mining crystal for each ore.
- Ice: some systems have an Ice Field by a cold planet. Its chunks (Rime, Glacial, and Dark Ice in lawless space) are cut only with an Ice Harvester or a Strip Ice Harvester, and refine a block at a time into Heavy Water, Liquid Oxygen and Cryo Salts, which fuel blocks are now partly made of.
- Fleet and capital modules: Command Bursts (shield, armour, skirmish, information, mining) that boost every ship of yours nearby; the Siege Module for dreadnoughts and the Triage Module for carriers, which root the ship and burn Cryo Salts; the Industrial Core for capital industrials, doubling its bursts' reach; and the Interdiction Field Launcher for destroyers, whose fields stop everyone else warping out for two minutes.
- Every human people now makes its own version of most utility modules (propulsion, tank, fitting, weapon upgrades, electronic warfare, remote repair, drone upgrades), shaded to its style: Vessari easier to fit, Talmar reaching further, Orun strongest but hardest to fit, Kethri lightest on capacitor.
- The pirate site tier: Unbound Raider, Reaver and Warlord makes of most modules, better than any people's make and short of an officer's. Found only in pirate dens' and strongholds' stores, camp chiefs' wrecks and pirate tower commanders' wrecks; the deeper or more dangerous the place, the higher the grade.
- Ice fields mix ordinary ore rocks among the ice, and the ice comes in two looks: soft dark blue chunks (Glacial and Dark Ice) and sharp pale blue crystal clusters (Rime Ice).

## Towers by their people

- A control tower is now its people's make. On top of the old figures, each people's tower is better at what its people are good at:
  - its gun batteries fire that people's kind of gun (projectile for the Vessari, Talmar and Unbound, hybrid for the Orun and Nimia, lasers for the Vail) and do 15% more damage;
  - its missile batteries do 15% more for the Vessari, Talmar and Unbound;
  - its own defence is 15 points better against every kind of damage: armour for the Vessari, Orun and Nimia, shield for the Talmar and Vail, structure for the Unbound.
- There are now Unbound, Nimia and Vail towers too. A people's tower is sold at its own stations; elsewhere a few are imported, at a higher price.
- A tower's information sheet lists what its make is better at.
- The Unbound's, the Nimia's, the Vail's and the Drowned's ships and towers now have blueprints.
- Unbound ship blueprints are found in the wrecks of pirate towers you destroy, and rarely on a contract board outside secure space (dear).
- Blueprint copies cost a tenth of what they did: ten runs cost about a tenth of one of the things they build.
- Blueprint originals cost half what they did: five times what they build.
- The events, guide, scanner and colonies windows are set out as the market is: what kind of thing down the left, the list in the middle, and the one picked on the right.
- In the inventory, pick several stacks at once: Shift-click picks everything from the last one picked to the one clicked, Ctrl+A picks everything shown, and a box dragged from empty space picks everything it touches (with Ctrl held, beside what was picked). A plain click on one of several picked picks it alone; dragging it carries them all.
- A ship you are not flying can be repackaged from the Ships window: what is fitted and carried goes into the hangar (rigs are lost, as when they are taken out), and the hull is a packaged hull again, to sell or put on a contract. It must be undamaged.
- The factions' wars can catch you now. Fire on one side's ships at a front, or take a war job for one side, and you are on its books: while that war lasts, and until you have mended your standing with the other side, the other side's patrols and police attack you in their space, their ships at the front go for you from the first, their stations will not let you dock, and where they blockade a system their pickets wait at its gates, one with a warp disruptor. A pilot who has worked for neither side is left alone.
- A system you hold pays you for its trade as well as the levy: the sales tax on what its markets sell, and transit dues on what goes along its lanes, every day with the levy.
- Your hired pilots and your corporation's members go round a pirate surge or an invasion when there is a way round, and never through a closed gate.
- Your corporation has a window of its own, from the dock's Corporation button, docked or in space: Overview (its account, paying in and taking out, what it makes and costs), Books, Members, Hangar and Journal. The Wallet is your own money alone.
- Assets: every place you have something down the left, with what is there worth; everything at the place picked (or everywhere) as one list, sortable, with a search over all of it; and the place on the right, with Set course.
- The star map is a window like the rest, its Factions, Security and Prices as tabs.
- Every window has a pin beside its close button: pinned, it cannot be moved or made another size until unpinned, and stays pinned from one game to the next.
- Your fleet's orders are a menu (Orders, on the fleet panel), each greyed with the reason where it cannot be given; a right click on a pilot's row gives the menu for that pilot alone. Engage stays a button of its own.
- A window opened beside the dock (the scanner, the colonies, the information) can be dragged again: it went back where it started.
- Information on an item is a window with tabs: description, attributes, requirements, industry and market (with a button to show it on the market). A ship on its description is drawn as it looks in space, in its base paint, as in the fitting window.
- A ship bought fitted out on a contract comes assembled, its modules fitted and its guns loaded, with the rest of its rounds in the hold. It waits at the station where it was posted.
- You cannot undock in a ship with a module fitted that you have not the skills for: the button says which.
- You can anchor a tower in wormhole space, at any depth. A claim beacon can't go on one there: nobody holds a system in wormhole space.
- Invasions out of wormhole space are the Drowned now, come up from their own depths through a great wormhole, led by a named Coffin captain. Pirates stood in for them before.
- Missile launchers turn to follow their target, as turrets do: the rack swings on its mount and tips up and down.
- Eight new skills, learned from books: Husbandry, Raising, Fire Control and Deep Boring for your towers and colonies (less fuel, quicker anchoring, harder-hitting batteries, more from extractors), and Hospitality, Renown, Muster and Stewardship for your corporation and holdings (more room for members, more applicants, more calls at once, more levy).
- Skills are in groups down the left of the Skills window (Piloting, Navigation, Weapons, Missiles and so on), each with how many of its skills you know.
- Blueprints of the alien peoples' ships and towers turn up on contract boards now and then: the Vail's and the Nimia's in their own space, the Drowned's anywhere outside secure space. They are dear.
- Freighters are as big as carriers and cost about as much (1.2 billion); jump freighters about 9.6 billion.
- Earnings are much higher, the more so the bigger the work: pirate bounties (a pirate battleship is worth 2.5 million), every kind of job, mining (lasers cut two and a half times as much, strip miners four times), pirate tags and the Drowned's alloy (five times what they sold for), and salvage (five times as much off a wreck). The aim is about five hours of the work a hull is for to buy the next one up.
- Named pirate officers leave a blueprint copy of an Unbound tower, good for a run or a few. Nobody has the original.
- Contracts are a window of their own on the dock, and galaxy-wide: every station's lots in one list, nearest first, which you can narrow by how far off and by what they are. Buy any of them from anywhere, docked or in space: what you buy is delivered to your hangar at the station it was posted at. A want is delivered to from anywhere too, from your hangar at that station.
- Your contract listings can be called off from anywhere; the lot goes back to the hangar where it was up.
- The contracts window no longer holds the game still when it opens, nor slows it while it is open: every board is gathered a little at a time from the start of a game, and if you open the window before that is done it says how far along it is.
- Markets differ now. A station has plenty of its own faction's ships, towers, guns and ammunition, and only a few of another human faction's, at a higher price.
- Blueprints are sold only for a station's own faction's things and for things nobody owns, and only at the kind of station that deals in them: ship prints at shipyards, module and ammunition prints at outposts, structure prints at refineries, all of them at trade hubs.
- Each kind of station keeps plenty of what it trades in (a refinery ore and minerals, a shipyard ships and modules, and so on) and a little of everything else. Trade hubs keep everything.

- The whole interface has been made over to one look: flat dark panels with thin edges, one accent colour, and colours that each mean one thing (green for yours and for a gain, amber for a warning or where you are going, red for hostile, a loss or a refusal).
- Down the left edge of the screen there is a dock: a button for every window, docked or in space. Hold the cursor on one to see what it is and the key that opens it. A button whose window is open is marked, and Jobs shows how many you have in hand. The clock is at its foot: stop and run, how fast, slower and faster.
- Every station service is a window of its own, opened from the dock: Fitting, Ships, Market, Industry, Jobs, Skills, Wallet, Crew and Corporation. The old docked screen is gone, and the station is seen behind the windows.

- The system, your ship and your wallet are in a panel of their own in the top left corner.

The list shows what the station asks and bids, what it has and what you have: click a column's head to sort by it. Double-click a module to fit it. The rigs are on the wheel. Charges for your fitted weapons are under the list: one button loads every weapon that takes them.
- Under the fitting wheel: how much of the hull's CPU, powergrid, calibration and hold the fit takes, repair, and "Strip the fit" (press twice), which sends every module back to the hangar and leaves the rigs. Paints and designs are on the Appearance page.
- The fitting window says where your capacitor holds with everything running, or how long it lasts.
- Skills open in space as well as docked. Pick a skill to see what it does, what its next level opens, what it needs and how long it takes. "Add up to level 5" queues every level left. The training queue is along the foot, and your implants are on a page of their own.
- Industry: blueprints, ore and running jobs on the left, and on the right the job the blueprint you picked would be, with the laboratory under it.
- Jobs open in space too: your jobs in hand, with how each is going and how long you can keep it. Docked, the window also has the jobs offered, the loyalty store and your standings.
- Ships: the ships you have at the station, with Board, and the hulls in the hangar waiting to be assembled.

A module is a button with its key, its rounds and how far through its cycle it is. Hold the cursor on one to see what it is and what it is doing.
- On a hull with more than eight modules in a row, weapons that are the same are one button with their number on it, switched on and off together.
- The overview has six presets as tabs: Default, Combat, Mining, Exploration, Travel, Salvaging. Each lists ships and what its name says. Whatever you have selected, locked or are travelling to is always listed. A row says what kind of thing it is and, for a ship, how fast it is going.
- A locked target's card shows how far off it is and how fast it is going. Your jobs in hand are listed under the corner panel while you are in space. Notices are coloured by what they are.

- Every people's stations have new towers (the Vessari's, the Talmar's, the Orun's, the Unbound's, the Vail's and the Nimia's): a small, a middling and a large one each, no two alike. Rings turn, dishes scan, panels follow the sun and hazard lamps blink.

## The Drowned, and jobs that pay

- Jobs pay five times the credits they did, every kind of them: a first assault on a pirate camp about 100,000 where it was 20,000, a bounty 45,000 a pirate where it was 9,000, and so on up the levels. Loyalty points are earned as they were.
- The last of the alien peoples: the Drowned, whose windowless ships are full of fluid, and whom nobody has seen alive. They hold nothing on the map. They are met in the two deepest levels of uncharted space, past where even pirates go.
- Fifteen ships of theirs, each with a lesser hull out on one flank that houses its cloak: from the frigates Cairn and Charon to the dreadnought The Reaper and the ultracapital The End.
- At a belt in the deep they come for you under their cloaks, unseen and unannounced until they are in reach. They hit harder than pirates and take longer to kill.
- Nobody pays a bounty on them. Their wrecks hold Benthic Alloy, what their hulls are made of, which sells for a great deal.
- The End, their ultracapital, has a railgun fixed along its bow. Lock a target and press BOW GUN (or G): the ship aligns to the target as it would to warp, and fires one enormous round when it is pointed. It hits capitals and towers squarely and misses small, fast ships.
- The Griever, their mining capital, has a refinery aboard: right-click ore in its hold and choose Refine aboard, in space or docked.
- A hull with a cloak of its own now draws on its capacitor while cloaked. It settles at about four fifths of full, and a ship with an empty capacitor cannot cloak.
- A new game has seven peoples holding space on the map, where there were eight. A save from before keeps its map as it was.

## The Vail's mining fleets, and a disruptor for capitals

- A capital ship is no longer held by an ordinary Warp Disruptor. It takes the new Capital Warp Disruptor, which only a capital can fit. That goes for your own capitals too.

## The Archangel, and a jump drive that takes a fleet with it

- The Vail's Archangel is their ultracapital now: half as big again as their capital industrial, the Embrace.
- Its jump drive has a field. When it jumps, every friendly ship within 5 km of its hull goes with it, and comes out standing about it as it stood. A ship in warp, or one with a warp scrambler on it, is left behind.
- The map's jump panel says how many ships are within the field, and a hull's sheet says if its drive has one.

## The Vail's ships

Frigates Prostration and Undulation, destroyers Exaltation, Adoration and Aberration, cruisers Enervation and Interstition, the attack battlecruiser Intervention, the battlecruiser Innocence, battleships Penance and Extravagance, the carrier Accordance, the dreadnought Awe, the mining barge Panacea, the freighter Indulgence, and the capital industrials Embrace and Archangel.

## A night's work

- The game opens on a splash: its mark and its name come up out of black, and any key or a click sends it on to the menu.
- Paint for your ship. Stations sell twelve paints and eight designs (Market, Paints). - The picture of your ship on the fitting screen no longer flashes wrong for a moment when the fit changes.
- New towers. Every station's tower is a new model, grown the way the ship generator grows hulls and stood on end: a spindle that tapers to a spire above and a tail below, with a lit mooring ring round its waist. The Vessari's are blocky and square, the Talmar's smooth and swelling, the Orun's thin spars with masts. Three sizes of each.
- The ship generator has two more styles to draw hulls in: triangular (a dagger of stepped decks with a conning tower) and asymmetrical (one side heavier than the other, often with an outrigger). No ship in the game is drawn in them yet: they are there to pick seeds from (`bin/launch hulls`, S for the style).
- More music: two more pieces for space, one more for a station and one more for a fight, and a piece of its own for space no chart shows. A mood now goes through its pieces in turn.
- Sounds for what had none: a cloak going on and coming off, a module coming out of a mutator, a deal done on a market, a lock in a derelict opened or lost, drones going out and coming back, armour and shield being mended, an afterburner lit, a salvager's cut, loot taken aboard, a mining laser at work, a capital ship breaking up, and a warning when your hull is nearly gone. Word of an invasion has a graver sound than the rest, and what begins too far away to be told of is no longer sounded at all. Uncharted space has an air of its own.

## Better makes of a module

- Each of the four peoples has its own make of what it is known for, and of one useful thing besides: Vessari launchers, armour and cargo extensions; Talmar autocannons, artillery, shields and tracking; Orun blasters, railguns, armour and engines; Kethri lasers, armour and capacitor. A people's make gives 15% more than the plain module (more than a Custom make, less than Tech 2), fits the same and needs no more skill. Their own stations sell a few, at about six times the price, to a pilot they think well of (standing 2 or better), and about one job in five at their stations pays in one instead of credits: the job's line says which.
- Officers. A named pirate captain that is sighted, the leader of an invasion and the commander of a pirate tower each carry modules of their own, with their name on them: better than a Masterwork in everything, and easier to fit. They are only ever found in that officer's wreck.
- Mutators. A mutator puts a module through once, and what comes out is another module: what it gives, how hard it is to fit, what it draws and how far it reaches are each thrown again, anywhere from a quarter worse to three tenths better. It cannot be undone and cannot be done twice. Right-click a module in a station's hangar and choose "Mutate" (a mutator has to be in the same hangar). Mutators come from officers' wrecks and from some derelicts.
- An officer's module and a mutated one are not sold on any market: what you find is what there is.

## Three things from play

- A ship sent somewhere from a standstill no longer snaps to face it: the hull comes round as it gets under way, quickly for a frigate and slowly for a big ship.
- Skill books: right-click a book in the inventory and choose "Learn this skill". It works from the hangar or from your hold, docked or in space, and if the skill needs another first the menu says which. (The Skills tab's Learn button now finds a book in your hold too.)
- The assets window has a "Set course" button beside every station that holds something of yours in another system.

## Things going on

- Pirates now mass where the galaxy says they would: in systems where order is thin and trade is rich, several at once across the map, instead of one surge at a time near you. Destroying pirates in a system holds a surge off, and ten destroyed during one breaks it.
- A surge that runs its course leaves that system a little less safe for three days. One that is broken leaves it a little safer.
- A new Events button at the foot of the screen opens a list of everything going on, nearest first: where, how many jumps, how long it has left, and a button to set course there. The star map rings each such system.
- Sightings, every hour or two within a few jumps: a named pirate captain with an escort and a wreck worth taking, a derelict hull already found for you, or a wandering trader whose rare stock is on a station's contract board while they stay.
- Luck at places, every few hours: a station's refinery, yards or laboratory out of use for a day, a gate closed for repairs (routes and the autopilot go round it), or a rich belt of rare ore.
- Invasions: now and then a force comes out of wormhole space into a system and digs in for four hours. Its lines are on the overview; fight it there and the system's keeper pays 400,000 when the last of them is gone. Left alone, it shuts the works of that system's stations and leaves the place less safe for days. (For now the invaders are pirates.)

## Trial 1 of 3 for the dark specks

- One of the three withdrawn changes is back by itself, to find which of them blacked the sky in warp on AMD cards: effects that only add light (the stars, the warp streaks, glows) now say outright that they change nothing of the surface under them. To look for: is the sky right in warp, and are the dark specks on bright things gone?

## The sky in warp, put back

- The last release's change for the dark specks made the whole sky go black in warp on some graphics cards (AMD's, on Windows). That change is withdrawn: the sky is as it was, and so, for now, are the specks. They will be gone for properly once it is known which part of the change the card took badly.

## A station's field is a wall

- Nothing shoots across a station's field, in or out. Inside it nothing outside can lock you, and you can lock nothing outside: a missile already on its way stops at the field.
- A station's batteries now stand outside its field, where they can shoot and be shot at. They are on the overview (Gun Battery, Missile Battery). One that is destroyed is rebuilt in two hours.
- To go inside, approach the station (Approach, or Q with it selected): you fly in to the tower, and are told when you cross the field. Docking still works from the field's edge, as before.
- Pirates cannot come in, and neither can you while the patrols have you marked: the field puts you out. Pirates who were after you go for the batteries, or circle the field firing on the tower, and give up after a few minutes.
- Your own tower's field is up while it has fuel, and stays up when the tower seals itself. Raiders shoot at it from outside.

## A frigate of your own, and windows that move

- A new pilot begins in a frigate of the people whose station they start at (at a Vessari one, an Akashi), cheaply fitted: one small gun, one light missile launcher and a plate of armour, with a slot left free, and the skills to fit all of it. The old Courier is no longer where anybody begins. (A game already begun keeps the ship it has.)
- A pilot who wakes with no ship is given a bare frigate of the people whose station it is. A ship that was given pays little when it is lost (12,000 credits, as before): ships you buy or build are insured for what they are.
- The docked screen and the star map are windows now: drag either by its heading to put it where you like. They stay where you leave them.
- Traders fly the haulers and freighters of the people whose space they are in, and no longer the plain hulls nobody's yards build.

## No more dark boxes

- On some graphics cards (AMD's, on Windows) ships and station modules sat in large dark rectangles. A few pixels were being worked out as something that is no number at all, and the glow that spreads bright light spread those too. They are now caught before they can spread, and the places they came from are mended.

## Mended for Windows, and an icon

- The star could not be drawn on some graphics cards (AMD's, on Windows), which stopped the game as it started: its shaders, and every other one, have been gone through for what such a driver refuses.
- If something still fails to draw, the game says so in `log.txt` and carries on, where before it stopped and waited.
- On a screen with Windows' text scaled up (125%, 150%), the game now uses the screen's real size.
- The game and its launcher have an icon of their own, on the desktop and in the taskbar.
- On Windows the game no longer opens a black console window beside itself. What it has to say is kept in `log.txt`, in the game's folder: send that along if something goes wrong.
- This page now says what changed in words meant for players.

## First release on the LAN

- Limitless Space installs and updates itself from this page's launcher: put the launcher in an empty folder and run it.
- Options, Window: choose how the game opens (automatic, fitted to the screen, or a window of 1920 by 1080 or 2560 by 1440). It takes effect the next time the game starts.
- The inventory is a window you can move and resize (the Inventory button, or I): drag things between your hold, the hangar and wrecks, right-click for what can be done with them, and open any place in a window of its own.
- Pilots who join your corporation can be seen at their work where you are: miners at the belts, haulers at the gates, and fighters who come when pirates go for you.
- Known for now: the interface is laid out for screens of 1920 by 1080 and larger, and crowds itself on smaller ones.
